Quick start

Chordially plays a whole chord from one key, in the way chord instruments such as the Roland J-6 do. Pick a pattern in the grid (bottom left) and press ▶ (or Space). Chordially plays its chord progression. Or play the keys yourself: the KeyStep, the Mac keyboard (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K), the piano on screen, or the 12 chord pads. Every key plays a chord, and the STYLE decides how it's played: held, arpeggiated, strummed or as a rhythm.

Everything is saved automatically within about 5 seconds: patterns, settings and MIDI learn. The AU insert is saved when you pick or bypass it, its own knob settings every 30 seconds and when you quit. There is no WRITE step.

The piano and the colours

lightThe notes of the chord that's selected now: four notes, the bass (left) and three above it. In CHORD mode, the key you press picks the chord; it isn't one of the chord's notes itself.
solidThe notes sounding right now. With STYLE OFF, all four light together. With an arpeggio or phrase, they light one by one in rhythm. A note that's still ringing when the chord has already changed stays solid until it ends.
barThe key(s) you're holding: on the KeyStep, the Mac keyboard, a pad or the piano.

The LCD shows the same in words. CHORD shows key C4 → Cadd9 (which key gave which chord) and the four note names underneath. While the sequencer is playing and you hold nothing, it shows from the sequencer.

Playing chords

CHORD on (default)

One key = one chord. The key you press is always the bass note. The chord set decides the chord on it: in the set "Pop Bright", the A key gives Am, the B key gives G/B. The octave you play in doesn't matter: every chord is voiced in the same register, so moving between any two chords moves each voice only a few semitones (real voice leading, not parallel root positions).

If you hold several keys, the newest key wins. Let go of it and the chord of the key you still hold comes back. Let go of everything and the chord stops (with STYLE OFF; phrase notes finish their length).

CHORD off

The notes you hold are the chord, up to the newest four. The phrase plays them like an arpeggiator, and the LCD names the chord they form.

Sustain pedal

Keeps the chord sounding after you let go, until you release the pedal.

The 12 chord pads

The 12 chords of the current set in the current key, in order from the set's first chord. Each pad shows the chord, its notes, and the key that plays it (note name and Mac key). Click and hold a pad to play it. The pad of the chord sounding now is outlined.

CHORD section

CHORD SET64 sets of 12 chords, by genre: pop, jazz, bossa, neo soul, gospel, lofi, funk, blues, house, techno, trance, psy, goa, EDM, synthwave, ambient, healing, cinematic, classical, modal and more. Use ◀ ▶, the mouse wheel, or click the name for the full list.
KEYMoves the whole set: −12 … +12 semitones. The set's first chord then lands on that key. For example, KEY D (+2) puts the "C" chord on the D key, and the D key plays it in D.
CHORDCHORD mode on / off (see above).
HOLDLatch: with HOLD on, the chord keeps playing after you let go of the key. The next key you press replaces it. Turn HOLD off to release it. Off by default; MIDI-learnable (a controller button or a CC above 63 turns it on).
VOICINGThe register of the three upper notes (centre F#4 by default). Lower is darker and warmer; higher is brighter. The bass sits low, around F2–E3 (OCTAVE moves it with the rest).

PHRASE section

STYLEHow the chord is played. OFF = the chord is simply held (while the key is held, or for the whole step). See the list of styles below.
VARIATION8 variations of each style.
GATEThe length of each phrase note, 10–100 % of its slot. Short = plucky.
GATE RNDRandomises every note's length around GATE (0 % = all equal, 100 % = very uneven). Makes arps and rhythms feel played rather than programmed.
SWINGDelays every second sixteenth, up to a triplet feel at 100 %.
STRUMThe time between the notes of a strum (the d / u steps of the STRUM styles), 0–80 ms.
OCTAVEMoves everything Chordially plays up or down by up to two octaves (the built-in synth and MIDI out).

The tempo, style, variation, gate, swing, chord set, key, voicing, sound, filter, volume and the delay / reverb amounts belong to the pattern, as on the J-6. Switch pattern and they all change with it.

SOUND section and AU insert

INSTRUMENTThe picker in the SOUND header. INTERNAL is the built-in synth (the default). Or pick any instrument plugin on your Mac: Chordially then plays it instead, sample-accurately, with everything else unchanged (phrases, AU insert, delay / reverb, MIDI out, the piano). EDIT opens the plugin's own window, and its sound is saved and loaded with it. With a plugin, the internal synth's controls (SOUND, ENVELOPE, ATTACK … RELEASE, EG INT, FILTER, RESO, SUB) are hidden because they don't apply to it; VOLUME, PAN and the EQ stay. Pick INTERNAL to go back. macOS hosts Audio Units (not VST): practically every Mac instrument installs an AU next to its VST3, and those AUs are listed.
SOUNDThe built-in synth's sounds, all chosen for playing chords: pads, strings, brass, keys and organs, plucks and stabs, and bells in the style of the classic 1980s polysynths. Every sound is level-matched (the loudest moment of a held chord is about −12 dB at VOLUME 100, never above −0.5 dBFS). Click the name for the list by category.
FILTER / RESO / SUBThe sound's own filter cutoff (shown in Hz), resonance and sub-oscillator level, on 0 … 127 knobs like the envelope: each sound loads with its own values; turn to change; double-click = back to the sound's value. The ranges are the usable ones (FILTER from about 80 Hz, RESO up to 90 %). SUB is dimmed on sounds without a sub oscillator. The SOUND, FILTER, RESO, SUB, EG INT and envelope settings belong to the pattern.
ATTACK, DECAY, SUSTAIN, RELEASEThe envelope in real values: ATTACK, DECAY and RELEASE in ms / s, SUSTAIN as a level in %. One ADSR shapes both the volume and the filter sweep. Picking a SOUND loads that sound's own envelope onto the knobs; turning a knob sets the value (kept with the pattern). Double-click returns to the sound's own value. The small ENVELOPE drawing next to SOUND shows the shape as set now: rise, fall to the sustain level, hold, release (times on a log scale so both short and long stages are visible).
EG INTHow far the envelope opens the cutoff, 0–100 %: low = a steady tone, high = a bright attack and a plucky sweep. Loaded with the sound like the ADSR.
ADJUST / EQ tabsUnder SOUND (with INTERNAL): ADJUST shows the envelope, EG INT, FILTER, RESO, SUB, VOLUME and PAN; EQ shows the 3-band EQ with a drawing of its curve, plus VOLUME and PAN. The tab you used last is remembered.
PAN, EQ LOW / MID / HIGHOn the chord sound, with the internal synth or a plugin instrument, after the AU insert: PAN (L / C / R) and a 3-band EQ (LOW shelf 200 Hz, MID 1 kHz, HIGH shelf 5 kHz, ±12 dB; 0 dB = flat). Kept with the pattern.
VOLUMEThe pattern's level. The factory patterns are matched so they play at an even loudness.
AU INSERTOne Audio Unit effect from your Mac, placed on the chord sound after the synth and before VOLUME and the delay / reverb sends. Pick it from the list. EDIT opens its own window; while that window is in front, your keys still play Chordially. BYP bypasses it. It gets Chordially's tempo and transport (tempo-synced delays follow), and it's saved with all its settings.

EFFECTS section

DELAY TIMESynced to the tempo: 1/16, 1/8T, 1/8, 1/8., 1/4, 1/4., 1/2. A ping-pong delay that darkens with each repeat.
DELAY / REVERBHow much of the chord sound goes to each effect (per pattern). The S-1 and T-8 have their own sends in their strips.
FEEDBACK, SIZE, TONEThe delay's repeats, and the reverb's length and brightness. These are shared by all patterns.

Patterns and the sequencer

Patterns

64 patterns: 8 banks × 8, from 1-1 to 8-8, all with factory progressions. Click one to select it. While playing, the new pattern blinks and starts when the current one reaches its end. CHANGE can set it to the next step or the next bar instead. Type a name in the name field. Use copy to … to copy the pattern to another slot.

Steps

Up to 64 steps. Each step holds a chord, a tie (the previous chord goes on, drawn as a line), or a rest (silence). Length sets how many steps play. Step sets how long one step is: 1/16, 1/8T, 1/8, 1/4 (default), 1/2 or 1 bar. The playing step is outlined in orange, and the LCD bar shows the page of 16 steps being played.

Click a stepSelects it (the cyan outline shows the cursor while EDIT is on). Alt-click (while stopped) plays its chord while you hold the mouse button.
EDITStep edit (while stopped): select a step, play a chord (any key, a pad or the piano), and it's written. The cursor moves to the next step.
TIEWrites a tie into the selected step and moves on: the chord before it is held longer.
RESTClears the selected step (silence) and moves on.
Step styleGives the selected step its own phrase style and variation, used for that chord and its ties. "pattern's" = the pattern's STYLE.
● RECReal-time recording while playing: the chord you play goes into the step that's playing (or the next one, if you press in the last quarter of a step).
CLEARDouble-click: empties the pattern (keeps its name and sound).
RANDOMWrites a new progression in the current chord set and key. The bass moves the way real progressions do (up a fourth / down a fifth strongest, then thirds and steps), the set's main chords come first, few chords repeat, and the version that flows best back to its first chord is kept. It also picks a rhythm (1- or 2-bar chords, pushes) and a phrase style. It names the pattern "Random NNN".
GENERATIVEOn: every time the pattern comes round, one or two chords are replaced by musically related ones, and now and then the variation changes. The first chord always stays, so the progression keeps its home while it evolves.

MASTER and the compressor

LEVELThe master output level. The meters show the chord sound, the output and the compressor's gain reduction (COMP, cyan, up to 20 dB).
COMPRESSOROn / off with the button next to its name. It works on the whole mix (chord sound, S-1, T-8, delay and reverb) before the output. THRESH −40…0 dB: the level where it starts. RATIO 1:1…20:1 (4:1 around the middle). ATTACK 0.1–100 ms: short catches peaks, longer lets the attack through. RELEASE 20 ms–1 s. MAKEUP 0–24 dB of gain afterwards. A soft knee keeps it smooth; after it, a soft limiter keeps the output below −1 dBFS.

S-1 and T-8 (USB)

Plug in the Roland S-1 and / or T-8 by USB. Chordially finds their USB audio by name (the strip's dot turns green) and mixes it in: LEVEL, MUTE, DELAY and REVERB sends. With SYNC on, their USB MIDI port gets Chordially's clock and PLAY / STOP (set them to follow USB MIDI clock). macOS asks once for microphone access, which is needed to hear USB audio: allow it.

AUDIOOn: the box's USB audio is mixed in. Off: Chordially doesn't open its audio at all (e.g. when you hear the box through a mixer instead).
MIDI INOff (default): nothing that comes from the box is taken: its keys play only the box, and its own PLAY / STOP / clock don't start Chordially. On: its keys also play the chords, and (with SETUP's transport / clock settings) it can start Chordially.
SYNCOff (default): the box gets nothing from Chordially and runs on its own (Roland boxes can start their sequencer on clock alone, so clock without start is not offered). On: it gets Chordially's clock and PLAY / STOP, and its sequencer runs in time with Chordially.
Audio menuAUTO takes the device named like the box ("S-1"), never an aggregate device and never one named for both boxes. If you use an aggregate device (Audio MIDI Setup, e.g. "S-1 and T-8" in one), pick it here for each box and choose its inputs: e.g. the S-1 strip in 1-2, the T-8 strip in 3-4. The status next to the name shows which device is used.
Thru → chNotes coming in on that MIDI channel (e.g. a KeyStep zone on channel 2) go straight to the box on its own channel, so you can play the S-1 from the KeyStep while Chordially plays the chords.

MIDI, KeyStep, clock (SETUP)

Chord notes out toA MIDI port and channel: everything the chord part plays is sent there, exactly as it sounds (phrases, strums). It can drive the MiniFreak, the SH-4d or a DAW.
Built-in synthOFF = only the external synth plays.
Keys in on channelALL = any channel, except the S-1 / T-8 THRU channels.
ClockINTERNAL: Chordially is the master and sends clock to every port listed under Clock out to (on by default, except the IAC bus). EXTERNAL: it follows incoming MIDI clock (the LCD badge turns EXT).
Start / stop inThe KeyStep's PLAY / STOP start and stop Chordially.
Program change inSelects patterns 1–64.
MIDI LEARNClick LEARN next to a parameter, then turn a knob on any controller. Or Alt-click any knob on the panel (every knob can be learned) and turn the controller knob. Defaults: the KeyStep 37's four knobs (CC 74, 71, 76, 77) = FILTER, STYLE, VARIATION, TEMPO. KEYSTEP DEFAULTS restores them.

Leave the KeyStep's own CHORD mode off. Chordially makes the chords.

Skins

SETUP → SKIN changes the look; it is saved. STUDIO: dark with orange (the J-6 feel). PAPER BLUE: soft off-white with calm blue, easy on the eyes in daylight. ARCTIC: brighter white with deep navy blue and a dark display. MIDNIGHT: deep navy with blue light. GRAPHITE: neutral grey with blue. PAPYRUS: warm cream paper with ink blue and a navy display, the softest for long sessions. The piano's three marks keep their meaning in every skin: light fill = chord notes, solid = sounding, bar = the key you hold.

Knobs, keys and shortcuts

KnobsDrag up / down (with Shift: fine), the mouse wheel steps by 1, double-click returns to the default, Alt-click = MIDI learn. The LCD shows the value while you turn.
TempoDrag the tempo field up / down (Shift: fine) or use the wheel. 40–300 BPM, stored in the pattern.
Mac keysA C · W C# · S D · E D# · D E · F F · T F# · G G · Y G# · H A · U A# · J B · K C.
Z / X move the Mac keys an octave down / up (this matters with CHORD off; with CHORD on, every octave plays the same chord).
TransportSpace play / stop. ■ stops; pressed again while stopped, it sends all notes off. Esc = stop (or close this window).
